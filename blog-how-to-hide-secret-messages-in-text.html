How to Hide Secret Messages Inside Ordinary Text (Steganography Made Simple)

By Secret Hider Team • Published: November 2026 • 5 min read

The Art of Hiding in Plain Sight

Have you ever wanted to share private information—like a password, an intimate confession, a surprise party plan, or confidential notes—without anyone looking over your shoulder realizing what you sent?

Traditionally, people use cryptography to encrypt messages into scrambled strings of random characters (like AES-256: 7f8b9a2c...). While secure, encrypted strings scream: "I have a secret!". Anyone intercepting the message immediately knows something hidden exists.

This is where Steganography changes everything. Rather than scrambling text into suspicious gibberish, steganography conceals the secret inside an innocent, ordinary sentence or emoji. To an onlooker, it just looks like you said: "Hey, what are you doing tonight?"

💡 Key Distinction: Cryptography hides the meaning of a message. Steganography hides the very existence of the message itself.

How Text Steganography Actually Works

Computers read text using universal character encoding known as Unicode. While most characters represent visible letters (like 'A', 'b', '3'), the Unicode standard also includes invisible non-printing characters known as Zero-Width Characters:

  • Zero-Width Space (ZWSP - U+200B): Takes up 0 pixels of width on screen.
  • Zero-Width Non-Joiner (ZWNJ - U+200C): Used for typographic ligature separation, completely invisible.
  • Zero-Width Joiner (ZWJ - U+200D): Connects characters or creates compound emojis, completely invisible.

By assigning binary values (such as ZWSP = 0 and ZWNJ = 1), any secret text can be converted into binary bits and tucked seamlessly between words or inside a single emoji!
